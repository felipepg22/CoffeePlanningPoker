# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Teams running live planning poker sessions to estimate tasks collaboratively. Users are facilitators and participants in a focused meeting context: they need to create or join a room, add or select tasks, vote quickly, reveal estimates, discuss differences, and save the final estimate without losing session flow.

## Product Purpose

CoffePlanningPoker exists to make collaborative estimation fast, clear, and resilient. Success means a team can run the core planning poker workflow from the first screen, recover predictably after refresh or reconnect, understand the current room state at a glance, and complete estimates with minimal instruction.

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, collaborative. The interface should feel friendly and sometimes lightly comic, with a coffee-session flavor that supports team energy without turning the app into a novelty. The product should still read as a reliable work tool: concise, responsive, and calm under live meeting pressure.

## Anti-references

This should not feel like a marketing landing page, a casino poker table, a toy-like card game, or a generic SaaS hero page. Avoid cartoonish clutter, decorative card grids, heavy dashboard gloom, and visual jokes that compete with voting, task selection, or facilitation.

## Design Principles

- Start in the workflow: the first screen should be usable app UI, not a sales page.
- Keep the room state obvious: participants, selected task, voting progress, reveal state, and final estimate should be easy to parse at a glance.
- Make playfulness functional: coffee and comic touches should reduce meeting tension or clarify state, never obscure controls.
- Preserve live-session speed: interactions should be quick, predictable, keyboard-accessible, and tolerant of refresh or reconnect.
- Write task-focused copy: labels should be short, specific, and only explain when the user would otherwise be blocked.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast and interaction states. Support visible focus states, keyboard navigation for room flow and voting, semantic controls, reduced-motion preferences, and layouts that prevent text overlap or control overflow on mobile and desktop.
